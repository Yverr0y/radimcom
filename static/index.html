ACARS
SCROLL PAUSED ACARSCPDLCADS-C

The audio of the VFO is muted while this decoder runs. Classic ACARS on the VHF airband (AM, 2400 bit/s). Works in any mode: the decoder demodulates AM itself. The buttons tune the European ACARS channels; 131.725 MHz is the busiest. VDL Mode 2 (digital, 31.5 kbit/s): the VFO runs at 2.1 MS/s around 136.500 MHz and all channels of the selected region (EU or US) are decoded at once; the frequency button re-centres the VFO. Most airline ACARS over Europe uses VDL2, and European ATN CPDLC exists only here. Tags: ACARS plain ACARS message, CPDLC controller–pilot messages (↑ from ATC, ↓ from the aircraft; FANS in ACARS, or the European ATN CPDLC on VDL2), ADS-C automatic position reports (FANS in ACARS, or ATN on VDL2). Click a tag at the top of the card to show only those messages (CPDLC = FANS and ATN CPDLC). MIN / MRN = message number / number of the message it answers. ✗ = CRC error. A message appears once its ADS-B data and flag have arrived, or after about 15 s without them. Blue = aircraft type, flight level and speed looked up on ADS-B (adsb.fi / adsb.lol, needs internet); click the callsign or flight to open the map. Country = from the aircraft's ICAO address. A click on a message marks it and holds the list still (SCROLL PAUSED); the mouse wheel over the messages resumes it.

Format (IOC from the start tone, LPM, line start from phasing) and fine tuning (AFC ±3 kHz) are detected automatically. Finished images are saved to the data\wefax folder. ISM

Devices on 433 and 868 MHz: weather sensors, remote controls, tyre sensors and more, decoded by rtl_433. The VFO runs at 2.048 MS/s, so everything within about ±1 MHz of the centre is decoded at once, and its audio is muted. The buttons centre the VFO. Gain stays the VFO's: a nearby sensor can overload the dongle at high gain. RTL-SDR only.

SSTV

Decodes the demodulated audio (USB/LSB on HF, FM e.g. ISS 145.800). Mode and start from the VIS code; without VIS the image starts from the line sync pulses. Fine tuning (AFC) and slant are corrected automatically. Use a filter of at least 2.7 kHz. Finished images are saved to the data\sstv folder.

SET · Settings

Settings for

Each VFO keeps its own settings. Dongles and VFO assignment are in data\vfos.json.

Dongle

Switching closes the dongle and opens the selected one. Each dongle remembers its own upconverter, LO and ppm.

Upconverter

Auto: below 30 MHz via the converter, VHF/UHF direct.

Above 30 MHz the signal must go straight to the dongle (bypass switch on the converter or another antenna).

Tuner gain

Digital gain in the RTL2832 after the A/D converter, independent of the tuner gain. Helps weak signals at low tuner gain, but can overload with strong ones.

Width
Suppression

Removes the peak of the dongle in the centre of the band, from the waterfall and from the received signal. Far left only takes out the peak itself; a wider setting also takes out its skirts (LimeSDR needs this), but weakens signals that close to the centre. Suppression sets how deep the removal goes. Each VFO keeps its own setting.

Edge
Shift
Depth

For spurs from local sources, such as a switching power supply: lines at a regular spacing of 12 kHz or more. LEARN looks at the band received now; learn on a quiet band, a row of steady stations could look like spurs. Other bands keep their lines. Each line gets a band-stop: flat over Width, sides as wide as Edge (far left = steepest), moved up or down by Shift (all lines together). Inside it the line is taken down to the noise around it, following its shape, by Depth at most. It follows changes of the gain within a few seconds. Cleans the waterfall and the channel you listen to (not WFM and wide FM). A signal on a spur is removed with it, so a clean waterfall does not mean it can be received there.

Only if the upconverter or LNA is powered by the dongle through the antenna connector.

Squelch

AM, FM and WFM each keep their own level; the slider sets the one for the current mode. Audio is muted while the signal is below the level (yellow mark on the S-meter). Far left = off. AM (airband): the carrier opens the squelch, so set it just above the noise level.

LPF – passes audio below (Hz)
HPF – passes audio above (Hz)

Audio filters of the LPF and HPF keys, for this VFO. Faster: turn the mouse wheel over the key. LPF 100–15000 Hz, HPF 20–5000 Hz, 36 dB per octave. Together they make a band-pass, so HPF always stays below LPF. What you hear and REC are filtered, decoders are not. Not for DAB.

AFI – inversion frequency (Hz)

Audio frequency inversion (AFI key): the audio spectrum is mirrored around this frequency (f → 2·F − f, band 0 … 2·F). The same setting scrambles and descrambles. Default 1750 Hz (band up to 3500 Hz). 500–4000 Hz.
